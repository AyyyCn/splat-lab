SPLAT LAB · THREE.JS + SPARK

Gaussian splats, photoreal three.js, and a game that runs in all of it

Browser experiments from a study of photorealism on the web: relighting splats, path tracing scans, AI-generated worlds, real scans as levels, and a bodycam game built on top of each. Everything runs locally from this folder; big assets stream from their own hosts.

python3 -m http.server 8771then open localhost:8771. A recent Chrome on a desktop GPU is best; the WebGPU page needs WebGPU.

Splat techniques

Lights out: relighting baked splats

Generated worlds come with their daylight baked in. A per-splat GPU pass turns it down to moonlight and adds a torch: beam cone, falloff, facing from each splat's shape, and shadows from the collision mesh. Press N for night, F for the torch.

  • Spark dyno modifier
  • per-splat lighting
  • shadow map from collider

Shoot the walls: splat debris

Each shot cuts the splats around the hit into small cells that fly off as rigid chunks, tumble, land and stay, with dust and a scorched crater. Worked out from the time since the shot, so it costs nothing once settled. Click to fire.

  • analytic debris
  • hash-grid raycast
  • 32 impacts

Portal hop: one world inside another

A ring in the village shows a second world; walk through it and back. Two passes with Spark's disk-clipping shader, each world drawn only in its own pass, and the torch shines through.

Scans as levels

Splat files with no collision mesh still play: the page levels the scan, builds collision from its splats (10 cm voxels, floor holes patched) and picks a start with room to move. Works for phone scans, SOG and compressed PLY.

  • collision from splats
