What to look for
  1. Watch a reset. Move the camera: a blurry low-res preview, a second of grainy samples, then the denoiser swaps in a clean frame. Hold C to see the raw samples it started from.
  2. Drop the samples to 4 or 8. Still clean at a glance, but thin twigs, petals and the gravel texture smear first: the denoiser invents detail it never saw. At 64+ it has less to invent.
  3. Try the standard network. Three times the weights; a little sharper on fine texture, slower per pass.
  4. Open the WebGL page at the same view. Without a denoiser it is still grainy in the shadows after 230 samples (about 40 s), where this page is clean after 32 (about 3 s).
Models, textures and skies: Poly Haven (CC0), all from Namaqualand, South Africa. Renderer: three-gpu-pathtracer 0.0.27 (WebGPUPathTracer) on three.js 0.186 WebGPU, denoiser: oidn-web 0.4 with Intel Open Image Denoise 2.3 weights.
